What is a Host Home?

A host home is a private residence where a trained, licensed provider shares their home and daily life with an individual with a developmental disability. It's an alternative to group homes or institutional settings — offering family-style living in a real community.

Host home providers are thoroughly screened, trained, and supported by Affinity Home Care Co. The individual with a disability is treated as a full member of the household.

Who Is Eligible?

  • ✓
    Adult or child with a diagnosed developmental disability
  • ✓
    Enrolled in a Colorado Medicaid waiver program (SLS, DD, CMHS, or CES)
  • ✓
    Assessed as needing community-based residential support
  • ✓
    Referred by a case manager or self-referred with waiver documentation

A group home typically houses multiple individuals with staff who rotate shifts. A host home is a private family residence where one (sometimes two) individuals live alongside the provider as part of their household. Host homes are widely regarded as more personal, stable, and community-integrated than group home settings.
There is no out-of-pocket cost to the individual or family. All host home placements through Affinity Home Care Co. are funded through Colorado Medicaid waiver programs.
